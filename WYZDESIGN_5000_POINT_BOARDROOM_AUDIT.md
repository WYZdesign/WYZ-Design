# WYZ Design 5,000-Point Boardroom Audit

**Owner:** Torreé Marcel  
**Operating rule:** evidence over optimism  
**Status:** living audit, initialized 2026-10-04

## Purpose

This is the decision system for making WYZ Design credible, polished, and profitable. It does not award points for intent, a feature existing in source code, or a promising idea. A point is earned only when the relevant customer, operational, financial, or technical outcome has current evidence.

The audit has **20 pillars × 250 points = 5,000 points**. Each pillar has five 50-point subcategories. Every subcategory is assessed as:

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0–9 | absent, misleading, or unsafe |
| 10–24 | exists but unreliable, unclear, or unverified |
| 25–39 | usable with material gaps |
| 40–44 | strong and verified |
| 45–50 | repeatable, measured, and ready to scale |

## Current executive read

The site’s engineering and mobile-polish program is moving quickly: current TypeScript, lint, and 15-test gates pass; recent 320px/360px production checks show no horizontal overflow on the sampled public routes. The business is not yet a 10/10 because proof, pricing canon, merch purchase completion, gift-card redemption, analytics, and verified client outcomes remain incomplete or owner-dependent. This audit is deliberately not assigned a final total until each subcategory has fresh evidence.

## 5,000-point rubric

### 1. Brand thesis and positioning — 250

- 50: One-sentence category and promise
- 50: Distinct visual and verbal identity
- 50: Clear differentiation from independent freelancers and generic agencies
- 50: Consistent service naming and hierarchy
- 50: Claim-to-proof traceability

### 2. Founder authority and story — 250

- 50: Official founder identity is consistent: Torreé Marcel
- 50: Founder origin story is concise and believable
- 50: Founder expertise is tied to client outcomes
- 50: Public social and press surfaces are current
- 50: Founder capacity and contact expectations are clear

### 3. Audience and market focus — 250

- 50: Priority customer segments are named
- 50: Los Angeles and any other active market claims are current
- 50: Buyer pain points are specific
- 50: Ideal project size and fit are explicit
- 50: Non-ideal work is filtered respectfully

### 4. Offer architecture — 250

- 50: Services have outcome-first descriptions
- 50: Packages make tradeoffs legible
- 50: Custom work has a defined path
- 50: Cross-sells are relevant rather than distracting
- 50: Every primary CTA leads to a fitting next step

### 5. Pricing and unit economics — 250

- 50: One approved source of truth for prices
- 50: Billing cadence, deposits, cancellation, and taxes are unambiguous
- 50: Public prices match checkout and booking prices
- 50: Each offer has a target gross margin and delivery cost
- 50: Discounts, gift cards, and referrals have protected economics

### 6. Lead generation — 250

- 50: Search landing pages match real buyer intent
- 50: Portfolio content earns inquiry trust
- 50: Lead magnet has a measurable opt-in rate
- 50: Contact paths work for desktop, mobile, and assistive technology
- 50: Lead source is captured without harming completion

### 7. Conversion journey — 250

- 50: First-screen value and CTA are clear
- 50: Booking path has working availability and payment expectations
- 50: Contact form provides confirmation and appropriate follow-up
- 50: Plans have accurate terms and a legitimate purchase or inquiry path
- 50: No apparent storefront, cart, or checkout behavior is cosmetic

### 8. Client onboarding — 250

- 50: Confirmation is immediate and human
- 50: Discovery intake captures delivery-critical details
- 50: Scope, timeline, and revision policy are confirmed
- 50: Deposit, invoice, and payment status are clear
- 50: Client knows the next owner, action, and date

### 9. Service delivery quality — 250

- 50: Brief templates exist for each core service
- 50: Milestones and approval points are explicit
- 50: File delivery is reliable and organized
- 50: Revision rules are fair and visible
- 50: Quality assurance and final sign-off are measured

### 10. Retention and referrals — 250

- 50: Follow-up sequence is consented and useful
- 50: Zeal rewards are understandable and functional
- 50: Referral terms are simple and auditable
- 50: Repeat-service suggestions fit actual client history
- 50: Testimonials and referrals request permission appropriately

### 11. Portfolio and proof — 250

- 50: Work samples load quickly and have useful context
- 50: Case studies show objective, process, and result
- 50: Testimonials are attributable with consent
- 50: Claimed event/client/project counts have a canonical definition
- 50: Portfolio is curated for intended buyers, not merely volume

### 12. Merch and commerce — 250

- 50: Catalog labels accurately reflect whether items are purchasable
- 50: Product detail includes size, material, availability, and fulfillment expectations
- 50: Cart and checkout complete a real order or are not presented as active
- 50: Printful, payment, shipping, return, and support responsibilities are clear
- 50: Post-purchase confirmation and status path are tested

### 13. Gift cards and stored value — 250

- 50: Purchase amount and terms are truthful
- 50: Recipient receives a secure, usable credential
- 50: Redemption works for every promised service or merchandise path
- 50: Balance, expiry, refund, and partial-redemption rules are clear
- 50: Ledger, webhook, fraud, and support controls are tested

### 14. Client experience and accessibility — 250

- 50: Navigation, keyboard, focus, and dialogs are reliable
- 50: Content is understandable at mobile scale
- 50: Forms have labels, useful errors, and recovery states
- 50: Motion, contrast, image alternatives, and reduced-motion behavior are considered
- 50: Support, complaint, and recovery paths are humane

### 15. Mobile craft and performance — 250

- 50: 320px and 360px have no unintended horizontal overflow
- 50: Gutters, text wrapping, and action targets remain deliberate
- 50: Chat, cookie, nav, and footer never cover needed content
- 50: Third-party embeds fail gracefully and do not leave unexplained blank space
- 50: Loading, image, and animation cost are controlled

### 16. Trust, safety, and privacy — 250

- 50: Privacy copy accurately describes actual processors
- 50: WYZMiND claims match OpenRouter and any other data transfer
- 50: Terms, returns, shipping, and copyright surfaces align with operations
- 50: Security controls protect payments, forms, uploads, and webhooks
- 50: Status and incident communication are honest and useful

### 17. Operations and fulfillment — 250

- 50: Capacity, scheduling, and delivery workload are visible
- 50: Vendor and contractor expectations are documented
- 50: Files, assets, backups, and client records have ownership
- 50: Exceptions and rush work have a controlled path
- 50: Founder work is documented enough to delegate safely

### 18. Financial controls and reporting — 250

- 50: Monthly revenue by service line is visible
- 50: Gross margin by service and merch line is visible
- 50: Cash, receivables, refunds, and liabilities are current
- 50: CAC, conversion, and lifetime value have definitions
- 50: Board decisions use a regular dashboard, not intuition alone

### 19. Growth systems and measurement — 250

- 50: Analytics are consented, installed, and verified
- 50: Search Console and local presence are current
- 50: Campaigns have source, audience, offer, and outcome tracking
- 50: Email and social sequences are measured for quality, not vanity
- 50: Experiments have hypotheses and stop/scale criteria

### 20. Governance, resilience, and scale — 250

- 50: Critical accounts, domains, and secrets have clear ownership
- 50: Deploy, rollback, and incident practices are verified
- 50: Dependencies, vendors, and subscriptions are reviewed
- 50: Legal and tax obligations have an owner and calendar
- 50: Expansion decisions protect quality and cash

## Board cadence

1. Score only evidence collected in the current quarter.
2. Pick the three lowest high-leverage subcategories for the next 30 days.
3. Assign one owner, one measurable outcome, one due date, and one source of proof.
4. Re-score after proof exists. Never improve a score because a task was merely started.
